## 1. Scope & Method

This review is *not* a re-derivation of the constitutional compliance already
covered by the two Antigravity reviews in this directory. It is a
**first-hand account of working inside the library**, written from a single
sustained session that landed RP-2 (retirement of the conflated
`ContractDomain` / `PassResourceDomain` enums in favour of independent
`ExecutionUnit` × `Substrate` axes).

Everything asserted below is grounded in artifacts produced or inspected during
that session, and every claim names its evidence. Where I could not verify a
property, I say so rather than assert it.

Method used, deliberately: for each load-bearing claim I asked *"what would make
this claim false, and can I cheaply make it false?"* Two claims survived that
test and are recorded as verified (§3.2); one of my own documentation claims did
not survive it and was corrected in-tree (§4.3).

## 3. What Is Genuinely Strong

### 3.1 The verification layer does real work

The gates in this repo are not decorative. Three properties distinguish them:

1. **Negative tests are first-class.** `shs_renderer_contract_placement_negative_test`,
   `shs_renderer_pure_value_library_negative_test`, and `t0_gate_negative_probes`
   assert that *misuse is rejected*, not merely that correct use succeeds. A gate
   suite that only tests the happy path cannot detect a rule being widened.
2. **Prove-fail variants exist.** `t0_known_answer_prove_fail` and
   `t1_08_known_answer_prove_fail` assert the check *fails* when it should. This
   is the practice that separates "a test exists" from "the test has teeth", and
   almost no codebase does it.
3. **The gates answered my questions for me.** When I landed the RP-2
   warning→rejection promotion, I never had to argue whether the change was
   correct: I removed the rejection and the gate went red, then restored it and
   the gate went green (§3.2). The tree was the oracle.

### 3.2 A refactor I could actually prove

RP-2 touched 216 annotation sites. I verified the change two ways rather than
asserting it:

- **Semantics-preservation, proven independently of the new law.** I enumerated
  the retired relation and the new `render_domains_compatible` across *every*
  input pair and confirmed identical results. This proved the enum split changed
  no plan's verdict *by itself* — which in turn proved the rejection promotion
  was a *separate*, deliberate change that had to be labelled as such rather than
  smuggled in as "part of the refactor".
- **The new gate is prove-failed.** `hybrid_interop_legality` was confirmed to
  fail when the rejection is removed and pass when restored. My first attempt
  disabled the wrong `report_.valid = false;` (there are two — the hybrid block
  and the cycle check), which is exactly the kind of near-miss that would have
  produced a gate with no teeth had I not checked.

### 3.3 Proportional, well-labelled claims in the docs

The architecture doc states measured *sizes* ("`GPU`, 59 uses in
`pass_contract_registry.hpp`; `Software`, 62 in `pass_adapters.hpp`; three dead
values with 0 uses") instead of adjectives. That is what made RP-2 scoped and
cheap: I knew before starting that it was a mechanical re-annotation, not a
rewrite. Measured evidence in prose documents is a genuine engineering asset.

## 4. The Central Finding: *Auditable* ≠ *Refactorable*, and Auditability Decays

### 4.1 Traceability is only as good as its freshness

While landing RP-2 I found the loop leaking in three places simultaneously:

- **A red gate already at `HEAD`.** `shs_renderer_header_inventory_check` fails
  on a pristine, unmodified checkout. I proved this against a clean `git worktree`
  rather than trusting a claim, then proved RP-2's enum split was
  inventory-neutral (regenerated file **byte-identical** with and without it) —
  establishing that all the pre-existing drift came from previously-merged
  `shader_identity.hpp` consumer work, not from me. Regenerated; gate green.
- **A flaky gate.** `shs_renderer_lifecycle_semantics_tests` fails roughly 1 run
  in 25 on `thread_pool_shutdown_order`, from a `ThreadPoolJobSystem` race with
  zero render-path involvement.
- **Doc drift.** Docs described state that the tree had moved past (§4.3).

None of these is an architecture defect. All three are *process* defects: the
artifact (gate, doc) was correct and valuable, but nothing was keeping it
current. A gate nobody runs is a comment.

### 4.2 The design is better at *proving* a change than at *surfacing* the need for one

RP-2 was cheap for a specific reason: the conflation was **contained** — one
uniform literal in two files, ordinal-identical enums, no external consumers.
That is good hygiene, but it is not the same as the architecture *alerting*
anyone that one literal (`ContractDomain::Software`) silently meant two different
things ("host execution" AND "software realization"). Nothing in the traceability
machinery flagged it. **The owner caught that by ruling; the tooling did not.**

Generalised, and stated plainly because it is the most useful thing in this
review:

> This architecture is excellent at proving a change is safe **once someone has
> decided the change is needed**. It is materially weaker at telling you a change
> is needed. These are different capabilities, and the second one fails
> *silently*.

The 0-use dead enum values (`CPU`/`OpenGL`/`Vulkan`) are the same phenomenon: a
public header carried dead-but-plausible vocabulary for a long time, and only a
manual sweep found it.

### 4.3 Where the docs and the gates disagree, nothing is the arbiter

I corrected two **of my own** overclaims in-tree during this session —
"enforced by construction" (it is enforced by a check, not by the type system)
and "RP-2 is inventory-neutral" (true of the *enum split*, but my new gate test's
`#include` legitimately changed the inventory by one `tracked_consumers` line).
Both were caught by re-reading my own work against the tree.

The structural risk this exposes: docs here are authoritative and rich, gates are
authoritative and executable, and when they disagree **nothing decides**. On a
pristine `HEAD`, the docs imply green while the inventory gate is red. Rich
prose that can drift silently is a liability proportional to how much people
trust it.

## 5. Risk Register

| ID | Risk | Severity | Evidence | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **R1** | Verification freshness — gates and docs drift with no trigger to notice | **High** | Red `header_inventory_check` at pristine `HEAD`; docs describing stale state | Run gates on a hook/CI so an unrun red gate is impossible |
| **R2** | Flaky gate trains operators to ignore red | **High** | `thread_pool_shutdown_order` ~1/25 | Quarantine until fixed; flakiness inverts a gate's value |
| **R3** | No arbiter when docs and gates disagree | **Medium** | Docs imply green at a commit where a gate is red | Tie doc claims to gate names + measured numbers + a date; make gates the arbiter by running them |
| **R4** | Identity-namespace shape duplicated 3× | **Medium** | `PassId` open since 2026-09-18; `ShaderId` + presets still closed enums with count sentinels | Finish RP-3 and P3 box 6 second half as one shape (rule of two already invoked) |
| **R5** | Dead-but-plausible vocabulary persists in public headers | **Low** | Three `ContractDomain` values with 0 uses | Extend inventory-style checks to flag unreferenced enum values |

## 6. Recommendations, In Priority Order

1. **Close the loop before adding gates.** The existing caution in
   `remaining_todos_2026-09-18.md` — that "Enforcement actually runs" should be
   revisited *before* the render-path track adds more gates — is correct and
   should be honoured. A headless dynamism gate that never runs is opt-in
   evidence, not enforcement.
2. **Quarantine the flaky gate.** Until `thread_pool_shutdown_order` is fixed,
   its signal is negative: it teaches people that red is sometimes ignorable.
3. **Keep doc claims mechanically checkable.** Every strong claim should name its
   gate and its measured number, and carry a date. The existing style already
   does this well in places; making it uniform removes R3.
4. **Finish the identity unification as one shape.** RP-3 plus the preset
   namespaces should land as a single third instance of the `PassIdRegistry`
   pattern, not as three bespoke registries.
5. **An environment note.** During this review's own session, terminal echo in
   the environment was intermittently lossy — I nearly "fixed" two spacing
   artifacts (`doesdouble`, `thatresidual`) that a byte-level check proved never
   existed in the file. Any verification workflow that reads content through a
   pipe should confirm bytes, not echoes. This is a tooling caveat, recorded
   because it nearly produced a false edit.

---

## 7. Verdict

The library is **genuinely pleasant and safe to work in**, and that is a real
achievement: I was never lost, never guessing, and the tree always answered my
questions. The verification layer is the standout — prove-fail tests and
negative-first gates are rare, and they paid for themselves within a single
session.

But the credit belongs to the right property. This architecture is
**auditable**, and it is *refactorable once a change has been decided*. It is not
self-alerting: the conflation at the heart of RP-2 was found by its owner, not by
its tooling, and the freshest signal in the repo was a red gate that nobody had
run. Keep the verification loop closed — run what exists, quarantine what
flickers, and keep doc claims pinned to gate names and numbers — and this becomes
a genuinely strong system. Let it drift, and the documentation becomes the wish
rather than the record.
